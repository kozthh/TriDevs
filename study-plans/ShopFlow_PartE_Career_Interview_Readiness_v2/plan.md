# ShopFlow Part E - Career Interview Readiness

## Study Rhythm
- 6 study days per week
- 2 to 3 hours per day
- Day 7: retrospective, mock review, and catch-up

## Study Method
- Treat the PDF as an interview operating system, not a one-time checklist.
- Train the document in layers: story, evidence, practice, and repetition.
- End each week with one updated artifact you can reuse in a real interview loop.

## Week 1: Career readiness orientation
Goal: understand the document’s purpose, structure, and interview expectations.

Day 1:
- Read the headings and section titles.
- Map the document structure in your notes.
- Identify the main career-readiness themes.

Day 2:
- Read the opening sections carefully.
- Write what the curriculum expects you to prepare.
- Note the roles, outcomes, or hiring stages mentioned.

Day 3:
- Capture the main interview concepts in short definitions.
- Add unfamiliar terms to a glossary.
- Write a plain-language explanation for each term.

Day 4:
- Review any examples, templates, or sample answers.
- Explain what each example is showing.
- Note the structure of a strong response.

Day 5:
- Build a glossary for the PDF.
- Group terms by resume, portfolio, behavior, technical interview, networking, or follow-up.
- Mark the concepts that keep appearing.

Day 6:
- Recreate the document structure from memory.
- Give a 2-minute explanation of the curriculum’s purpose.
- List the sections that need a slower second pass.

Day 7 retrospective:
- What part of the interview process feels most important?
- Which concepts already feel familiar?
- Which ones still need concrete examples?
- What should the next week emphasize?

## Week 2: Resume, portfolio, and personal narrative
Goal: shape the materials that open the interview conversation.

Day 1:
- Revisit your week 1 notes.
- Read the resume and portfolio sections.
- Identify the document’s preferred framing.

Day 2:
- Focus on resume structure.
- Write the sections that belong on a strong resume.
- Capture any advice about clarity, brevity, or impact.

Day 3:
- Study bullet writing and achievement framing.
- Rewrite weak bullets into stronger action-result statements.
- Note any metrics or evidence patterns the PDF recommends.

Day 4:
- Review portfolio or project presentation guidance.
- Write how to explain one project in under two minutes.
- Capture the proof points a reviewer should notice.

Day 5:
- Compare different ways to describe the same experience.
- Note what makes one version stronger than another.
- Summarize the personal narrative lesson in a paragraph.

Day 6:
- Rebuild a resume checklist from memory.
- Recite your project story without looking at notes.
- Identify the weak spots.

Day 7 retrospective:
- Can I explain my experience clearly and briefly?
- What part of my resume still feels vague?
- Which project story needs more evidence?
- What should I tighten before the next week?

## Week 3: Interview performance and practice
Goal: turn preparation into repeatable interview behavior.

Day 1:
- Re-open the PDF sections on interviews and practice.
- Identify the behavioral and technical themes.
- List the terms that connect directly to interview performance.

Day 2:
- Study behavioral interview guidance carefully.
- Write the practical meaning of each recommendation.
- Turn each one into a checklist item.

Day 3:
- Focus on technical interview structure.
- Note how the PDF expects you to explain reasoning.
- Summarize those expectations in your own words.

Day 4:
- Review mock interview or rehearsal advice.
- Write down the patterns that make practice sessions useful.
- Mark any anti-patterns or warning signs.

Day 5:
- Look for communication, confidence, or problem-solving guidance.
- Summarize what helps answers feel clear and credible.
- Add any cautions or tradeoffs mentioned in the PDF.

Day 6:
- Run a closed-book recap of the interview topics.
- Rebuild a mock-interview checklist from memory.
- Write one paragraph explaining why these rules matter.

Day 7 retrospective:
- Which interview skills are most actionable?
- Which ones still feel abstract?
- What checklist item should I use by default?
- What example would help most next week?

## Week 4: Review, repetition, and job search readiness
Goal: compress the PDF into a reusable interview prep system.

Day 1:
- Review all prior notes.
- Identify the top career-readiness ideas to keep permanently.
- Mark the sections you still reread often.

Day 2:
- Re-read only the weakest sections.
- Rewrite them with simpler words.
- Remove duplicates and unclear phrasing.

Day 3:
- Create a final interview prep sheet.
- Include resume, portfolio, behavioral, and technical notes.
- Keep the list short and memorable.

Day 4:
- Run one full mock interview using the PDF guidance.
- Explain the answer structure and the evidence you use.
- Note how the practice session went.

Day 5:
- Do a full recall test from memory.
- Compare your recall to the source.
- Fix any mismatches in your notes.

Day 6:
- Write the final summary for this PDF.
- Capture the most important interview lessons.
- Add one concrete practice task for later.

Day 7 retrospective:
- Which interview habits are now automatic?
- What still needs more repetition?
- What summary note will help me start faster next time?
- What is the single most important lesson from this PDF?
