# ShopFlow Part 0 - Foundations

## Study Rhythm
- 6 study days per week
- 2 to 3 hours per day
- Day 7: retrospective, notes cleanup, and catch-up

## Study Method
- Day 1 of each week is for structure and preview.
- Days 2 to 5 are for focused reading and note-making.
- Day 6 is for recall, mini review, and closing gaps.
- Day 7 is for retrospective, correction, and planning the next cycle.

## Week 1: Orientation and map of the foundations
Goal: understand what this PDF is teaching, how it is organized, and which ideas matter most.

Day 1:
- Read the table of contents, headings, and subheadings.
- Write a rough map of the document in your notes.
- List every term that looks important or unfamiliar.

Day 2:
- Read the opening sections slowly.
- Summarize the main problem the PDF is trying to solve.
- Note any assumptions the material makes about the learner.

Day 3:
- Read the next section and highlight repeated ideas.
- Write each key idea in one sentence.
- Add one real-world example for each idea.

Day 4:
- Re-read the most foundational section.
- Convert the prose into short bullet notes.
- Record the relationships between terms, tools, and concepts.

Day 5:
- Review the section summaries from days 1 to 4.
- Quiz yourself without looking at the PDF.
- Mark the concepts that need a second pass.

Day 6:
- Revisit the hardest page or section.
- Write a short explanation of the PDF’s main foundation topic.
- Create a glossary page for the week.

Day 7 retrospective:
- What did I understand without rereading?
- Which terms still need a clear definition?
- What parts of the document felt like background versus core ideas?
- What should be reviewed first next week?

## Week 2: Core foundation concepts
Goal: turn the general introduction into precise understanding.

Day 1:
- Re-open your week 1 notes before reading.
- Read the next major section from start to finish.
- Write the section goal in your own words.

Day 2:
- Break the section into smaller idea clusters.
- For each cluster, write what it means and why it matters.
- Note where the PDF repeats earlier material.

Day 3:
- Focus on the examples, diagrams, or walkthroughs.
- Reproduce the logic of each example from memory.
- Add a short explanation underneath each example.

Day 4:
- Re-read the section and compare it to your notes.
- Correct vague wording and replace it with exact language.
- Capture any rules, patterns, or guidelines explicitly.

Day 5:
- Make a one-page summary of the section.
- Include definitions, flow, and the most important takeaways.
- Mark anything that is still confusing.

Day 6:
- Test recall by teaching the material aloud or in writing.
- Rebuild the section outline without looking at the PDF.
- List the weakest points for the retrospective.

Day 7 retrospective:
- Which ideas can I explain cleanly from memory?
- Which definitions need to be tightened?
- What should I stop rereading because it is already clear?
- What should I schedule for another pass?

## Week 3: Application and mental models
Goal: connect the foundations to practical understanding.

Day 1:
- Review all prior notes before opening the PDF.
- Identify the key mental model used in this part.
- Write the model as a simple flow or sequence.

Day 2:
- Read the next section with emphasis on cause and effect.
- Ask what happens first, what happens next, and what depends on what.
- Turn those relationships into arrows or numbered steps.

Day 3:
- Focus on any constraints, assumptions, or tradeoffs.
- Write the upside and downside of each choice mentioned.
- Note where the PDF prefers simplicity over completeness.

Day 4:
- Review the practical examples again.
- Reconstruct the example without checking the text first.
- Compare your reconstruction to the source.

Day 5:
- Build a small review sheet of the most reusable ideas.
- Highlight what is foundational versus what is just example-specific.
- Update your glossary if new terms appeared.

Day 6:
- Do a closed-book recap of the week.
- Write a short paragraph explaining the whole section.
- Flag any weak spots for the retrospective.

Day 7 retrospective:
- Which mental model is the most useful so far?
- Where do I still confuse one concept with another?
- What should become a flashcard or self-test item?
- What should be revisited in week 4?

## Week 4: Consolidation and recall
Goal: create a durable summary you can reuse later.

Day 1:
- Re-read all section summaries only.
- Identify missing gaps between your summaries.
- Fill those gaps with short notes.

Day 2:
- Create a final glossary for the PDF.
- Keep each definition short, exact, and plain.
- Remove duplicate or unclear definitions.

Day 3:
- Write a one-page document summary.
- Include the main theme, the key concepts, and the structure.
- Keep it short enough to reread quickly.

Day 4:
- Do a full recall test from memory.
- Rebuild the document outline without opening the PDF.
- Compare your outline to the actual structure.

Day 5:
- Review the weakest areas from the recall test.
- Read only those pages again.
- Rewrite those notes more clearly.

Day 6:
- Produce the final revision sheet for this PDF.
- Include key terms, major ideas, and your own warnings or reminders.
- Store one action item for later practice.

Day 7 retrospective:
- What is now automatic and what still needs effort?
- Which section deserves a deeper second cycle later?
- What summary note would help me most next time?
- What is the single biggest lesson from this PDF?
